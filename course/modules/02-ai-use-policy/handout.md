# A church AI use policy

By the end, you can write a one-page policy your pastor and board can review.

**Practice files (all FAKE):** board-notes.md, staff-survey.csv, current-computer-policy.md, from [FILES LINK].

**Do every step in the same chat.** Later prompts build on earlier ones.

## The four steps

1. New chat. Paste the files; ask what was decided and left open.
2. Ask for a draft, a rules table and open questions.
3. Move unsourced rules, and anything legal or denominational, to the open questions. Claude cannot give legal advice.
4. Check the draft against the table, each row against its source.

## Prompts to paste

Step 1, files pasted below:

```
I am drafting an AI use policy for our church office. Below are our board notes, our current computer and email policy and, if we have one, a staff survey. Do not write the policy yet. List what the board decided, what it left undecided, and what questions went unanswered.
```

Step 2:

```
Write a one-page draft AI use policy for our staff in plain words, headed "Draft for board review". Use only the board's decisions and our current policy; where they disagree, use the board's. Add a rules table: one row per rule, with the exact line it came from. Under it, list "Open questions [CONFIRM]": unanswered questions, anything undecided, and every place the notes and the policy disagree.
```

Step 3:

```
Check the draft again. Move any rule with no source line, and anything saying what the law or our denomination requires, to the open questions. Keep every unanswered question from the board notes there. For each, say who should answer: the pastor, the board or a lawyer.
```

Step 4 is yours, on a printout. Find each rule in the table; a rule with no row is an open question. Tick each row whose source line says the same. If the notes and the current policy disagree, the newer board decision wins; list it [CONFIRM].

## Decision-line check

- [ ] The top says “Draft for board review.”
- [ ] Every rule in the draft has a row in the table.
- [ ] Each source line is a board decision or current policy line saying the same as the rule.
- [ ] Nothing undecided became a rule.
- [ ] Where the sources disagree, the board decision is used, the conflict listed [CONFIRM].
- [ ] The law and denomination questions are under “Open questions [CONFIRM]”, with who answers each.

Practice with the FAKE files only. For your own, use roles for board members’ names, and leave out member names and prayer, health, children’s and giving details.
