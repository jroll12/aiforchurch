# A church AI use policy

By the end, you can write a one-page policy your pastor and board can review.

**Practice files (all FAKE):** board-notes.md, staff-survey.csv, current-computer-policy.md. Get them from [FILES LINK].

**Do every step in the same chat.** Steps 2 and 3 refer back to what Claude wrote before.

**What you take home is a draft.** Your pastor and board decide what it becomes. Claude cannot give legal advice.

## The four steps

1. Start a new chat. Paste the three files and ask what was decided and what was left open.
2. Ask for a one-page draft policy, a rules table and the open questions.
3. Tell Claude to take out any rule with no source, and anything about the law or your denomination, and list them as open questions marked [CONFIRM].
4. Check the draft against the rules table, then each row against its source. Then give the draft to your pastor and board.

## Prompts to paste

Step 1, with the files pasted below it:

```
I am drafting an AI use policy for our church office. Below are our board meeting notes, our current computer and email use policy and, if we have one, a staff survey. Do not write the policy yet. First list three things: what the board decided, what it discussed but did not decide, and what questions were left without an answer.
```

Step 2:

```
Write a one-page draft AI use policy for our staff, in plain words. Put "Draft for board review" at the top. Use only what the board decided and what our current policy says. If the board notes and the current policy disagree, use the board's decision. Then add a rules table: one row for every rule in the draft, with the exact line from the notes or the current policy it came from. Under the table, add a list headed "Open questions [CONFIRM]": every question left without an answer, everything discussed but not decided, and every place the board notes and the current policy disagree.
```

Step 3:

```
Check the draft again. Take out any rule that has no source line. Take out anything that says what the law or our denomination requires. Add each one to the "Open questions [CONFIRM]" list. Make sure the list still has every unanswered question from the board notes, including any about the law or our denomination. For each item, say who should answer it: the pastor, the board or a lawyer.
```

Step 4 is yours. Print the draft or copy it into a document.

- Find every rule in the draft in the rules table. A rule with no row goes on the open questions list.
- Read each row against its source: the board notes for a decision, the current policy for a carried-over line. Tick it when the line says the same thing.
- If the board notes and the current policy disagree, the newer board decision wins. List the conflict [CONFIRM].

## Decision-line check

- [ ] The top says “Draft for board review.”
- [ ] Every rule in the draft has a row in the rules table.
- [ ] Each source line is a board decision or a line in the current policy, and says the same as the rule.
- [ ] Nothing discussed but not decided became a rule.
- [ ] Where the two sources disagree, the board decision is used and the conflict is listed [CONFIRM].
- [ ] The law and denomination questions are listed under “Open questions [CONFIRM]”, each with who should answer it.

Practice with the FAKE files only. For your own policy, paste board decisions and your current policy with board members’ names replaced by roles, and leave out member names, prayer requests, health details, children’s details and giving information.
